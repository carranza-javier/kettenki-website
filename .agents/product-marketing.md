## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Custom software for Swiss SMEs — AI assistants, websites and apps — where a working
version exists before anyone commits.

**What it does:** KettenKI builds three things for small and medium Swiss businesses: AI chatbots and
assistants, websites, and custom-built apps. The work starts by clarifying the requirement and
shipping a first working version; only if that version convinces does the engagement continue.

**Product category:** **Individuelle Softwareentwicklung für KMU** (custom software development for
SMEs) / EN: *custom software development for small and medium businesses*.

> **Category decision — this was open and is now settled. Read the reasoning before changing it.**
> Neither "Digitalagentur" nor "Boutique(-Agentur)" is used, and neither should be reintroduced:
>
> 1. **"Agentur" sets up a comparison KettenKI loses.** It puts us on the shelf next to 10–50 person
>    agencies in Bern and the Mittelland, where buyers compare breadth (branding, SEA, social),
>    capacity and account teams. We are strong on none of those and it is not the fight we want.
> 2. **It contradicts how the one-person structure is communicated.** An agency implies account
>    managers and hand-offs. Our line is direct contact with no intermediaries; "Agentur" quietly
>    promises the opposite.
> 3. **It makes the differentiator look implausible.** "We build first, you decide after, no cost if
>    it doesn't convince" is credible from one engineer who ships fast. From an agency it reads as
>    desperate or as a bait offer, because agencies visibly cannot afford it.
> 4. **"Boutique" is not a word Swiss SME owners search or say.** It is design/branding jargon. A
>    physiotherapy clinic or a café owner searches "Webentwicklung Bern", not "Boutique-Agentur".
>    The site's own keywords already sit on the software-development shelf.
>
> The category stays deliberately plain. The differentiation is carried by the offer, not by an
> inventive label — which also matches the site's sober tone.

**Product type:** Service (custom development), plus three named in-house AI prototypes used as
demonstrators: **BAMBERA**, **LIVIANA**, **FANDANGO**.

**Business model:** Project-based custom development. The three named prototypes are free to test,
functional and tested, **but explicitly without warranty or SLA** — this disclaimer is on the site
and must never be dropped from any copy that promotes them.
`services_disclaimer_text` (DE): *"Diese Lösungen sind experimentelle Prototypen. Sie sind kostenlos
zum Testen, funktional und getestet, aber ohne Garantie oder SLA. Bei Erfolg: Gemeinsame
Weiterentwicklung möglich."*

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Swiss SMEs (KMU), German-speaking Switzerland, Bern and surroundings as the
local-search core. Sectors already named on the site: hospitality/gastronomy, industry and trade with
complex operating manuals, childcare, fitness studios, medical practices and clinics, professional
services, wellness, logistics.

**Decision-makers:** The owner or managing director. In a Swiss SME this is usually one person who is
simultaneously the user, the champion, the decision maker and the budget holder — there is no
committee. Occasionally a practice manager or operations lead is the day-to-day contact.

**Primary use case:** A small business loses hours to work a piece of software should absorb —
answering the same customer questions, hunting through a manual, retyping unstructured messages into
a system, or living with a website that no longer represents them.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Stop answering the same question twenty times a week."
- "Let my staff find the answer themselves instead of asking me."
- "Give me a website that looks like we're a serious business today."
- "Turn the mess of messages/emails/voice notes into something my system can read."

**Use cases (verbatim from the site, DE):**
- Gastronomy: *"Welche Gerichte enthalten Haselnüsse?"* → the assistant answers from the manual.
- Industry: *"Maschine zeigt Fehler E347, was tun?"* → step-by-step plus the service contact.
- Fitness: *"Kann ich eine Klasse vor der Anmeldung ausprobieren?"* → answered 24/7 on the website.
- Logistics: a delayed-shipment message becomes classified, structured data.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** The business runs on knowledge and processes trapped in people's heads, in a PDF
manual, or in an inbox — and the owner absorbs the cost personally, in evenings and interruptions.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **Agencies:** months of lead time, a proposal before anything exists, and a result the client
  cannot change afterwards without going back to them.
- **Website builders (Wix, Squarespace, Jimdo):** fine until the business needs something specific;
  then there is no path forward, and the owner is doing the work themselves anyway.
- **Off-the-shelf chatbot SaaS:** a subscription plus a configuration project, and it still knows
  nothing about this business's actual manual.
- **Doing nothing:** the default and the strongest competitor. It is free today and expensive slowly.

**What it costs them:** Owner hours that should go to the business. Slow staff onboarding. Enquiries
that go unanswered outside opening times. A web presence that quietly loses work to competitors.

**Emotional tension:** Not wanting to look naive about AI, and not wanting to be sold to. Suspicion
that it will be expensive, take forever, and leave them dependent on a supplier they cannot read.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Local Digitalagenturen in Bern / the Mittelland, and freelance web developers. Agencies
fall short on lead time, on price transparency and on leaving the client dependent; freelancers vary
wildly and often disappear mid-project.

**Secondary:** Website builders and off-the-shelf chatbot SaaS. They fall short the moment the
business needs something that is specifically theirs, and they never absorb the company's own
operational knowledge.

**Indirect:** Doing nothing, a relative who "knows computers", or a spreadsheet plus manual effort.
Falls short invisibly — there is no bill, so the cost never appears anywhere.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
1. **"Erst entwickeln, dann entscheiden."** A working version exists before any commitment. If it
   doesn't convince, no costs arise.
2. **Direct contact, no intermediaries.** The person who talks to the client is the person who
   builds. No account layer, no hand-off, no brief lost in translation.
3. **Real engineering depth, not a template shop.** Backend/full-stack engineering on international
   projects, and AI built with attention to security and legal compliance
   (*"Wir legen grossen Wert auf Sicherheit und die Einhaltung gesetzlicher Vorschriften"*).
4. **Multilingual by default** — the assistants work in DE, FR, IT, EN, ES, which matters in
   Switzerland and is rarely offered at this size.

**How we do it differently:** We invert the usual order. The industry sells a proposal and then
builds. We build, then talk.

**Why that's better:** It moves the risk off the client. They judge something real instead of a
promise, and they can say no at the point where saying no is still free.

**Why customers choose us:** Because they can find out whether it works before it costs them
anything, and because the person who understood the problem is the person who solved it.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Sober, plain, understated. Claims are small and true. The site deliberately avoids
advertising register; a single slogan-shaped sentence stands out as foreign and should be resisted.

**Style:** Direct and concrete. Short sentences. Says what happens, in order, without adjectives.
Addresses the reader as **du**. Speaks as **wir**; **ich** only where a person is genuinely the
subject.

**Personality:** Precise · calm · honest · unhurried · competent.

The founder's stated stance, and the register to hold (`about.html`, DE): *"Javier Carranza ist ein
Softwareingenieur, der bewusst abseits des Lärms und der Eile arbeitet, die die heutige
Technologiewelt dominieren."* and *"Er glaubt an Einfachheit, Qualität und langfristiges Denken."*

**Brand slogan — never change, never translate freshly, never A/B test:**
- DE: **"Warum weniger tun, wenn man mehr tun kann"**
- EN: **"Why do less when more is possible"**

**Two audiences, two voices — do not blend them.** The commercial site (`index`, `services`, `about`,
`contact`) is dark-themed and speaks to SME customers. `portfolio/` and `blog/` are light-themed,
use Inter, are deliberately kept out of the main nav, and address recruiters and a technical
audience. Marketing work aimed at customers must not be applied to portfolio or blog pages, and the
site chatbot speaks as KettenKI the company — never as Javier the job candidate.

## Page notes — `services.html` (Lösungen)

CRO pass run 2026-09-06 (`marketing-skills:cro`), measured in-browser. **No analytics exist, so these
were reasoned from CRO principles plus scroll measurements, not from this site's data.** Treat the
result as a hypothesis that has never been validated.

   are free anyway.

   plus a subtitle in the existing unused `.section-subtitle` (no new CSS) carrying the
   differentiator.
3. Disclaimer de-escalated: amber gradient + 2px amber border + 🧪 + *"Wichtiger Hinweis"* → neutral
   surface, 1px standard border, heading *"Was diese Prototypen sind"*. **The body text is unchanged,
   word for word** — the honesty was never the problem, the volume was.
4. `Für wen` moved above the description in all three product cards; its separator moved from
   `border-top` to `border-bottom` to keep the rhythm. Self-identification now precedes explanation.
5. `Mehr erfahren` → `BAMBERA/LIVIANA/FANDANGO ansehen`. **New keys `cta_view_*` rather than editing

**Sections swapped 2026-09-07 — `Individuelle Entwicklung` now leads.** It is the work that produces

**The first contact CTA moved from 3.00 to 1.05 screens on desktop (1.22 on mobile)** — against 2.81

> **The form sends for real** (2026-09-07): API Gateway → Lambda → SES, stack `kettenki-contact` in

> that appears to succeed and discards the enquiry — for a business with one client, a silently

## Open questions
